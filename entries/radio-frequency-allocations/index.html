UK Radio Frequency Allocations

Primary source: Ofcom UK Frequency Allocation Table (UKFAT) · Mode subdivisions: RSGB Band Plan 2026 · Compiled 4 Nov 2026 · Antenna lengths computed at band centre

Every frequency band allocated in the UK, what it's used for, how the spectrum is divided between modes (CW, SSB, FM, digital protocols), and the corresponding antenna lengths (half-wave and quarter-wave dipole). The Useful Frequencies section covers the frequencies you actually want to listen to — ADS-B, GPS, weather, aviation, DMR, APRS, FT8, PSK31, RTTY, and UK broadcast.

Download print-optimized PDF (repeating table headers, 16 pp)
How to read the antenna columns: λ/2 = half-wave dipole total length (two equal elements, 180° apart, fed at centre). λ/4 = quarter-wave vertical monopole height (over ground plane) — the most common practical antenna. Values are for free space at the stated frequency; real-world shortening factor ≈ 95% for wire dipoles. For VHF+ FM work, a J-pole or 5/8-wave vertical is the usual recommendation over a bare λ/4.

03Known Frequencies Worth Tuning

Real, well-known frequencies across the bands for particular purposes — weather, number stations, time signals, emergency/distress, and international broadcasting. These are for listening (SDR or scanner) unless noted as ham emergency CoAs. Emergency frequencies are for receiving/monitoring; never transmit on them outside a genuine emergency.

Weather stations (NWS HF, worldwide SDR listening)

US National Weather Service HF weather — AM voice + digital subcarrier, receivable worldwide. The classic "weather station" band. UK has no equivalent HF service; the UK Met Office weather is on FM/TV/web.

04Mode & Protocol Glossary

ModeDescription
CWContinuous wave — Morse code (telegraphy). 200 Hz bandwidth, narrowest & most sensitive mode.
LSBLower Sideband SSB voice — used below 10 MHz (160/80/40 m). Dial setting = carrier, subtract ~3 kHz for tone.
USBUpper Sideband SSB voice — used above 10 MHz (30/20/17/15/12/10 m).
AM / DSBAmplitude Modulation, double sideband — conventional AM radio, aviation voice.
FMFrequency Modulation — 2m/70cm/6m simplex & repeaters, commercial FM radio, 12.5 kHz channels.
J-poleEnd-fed vertical: ¼λ radiator + ⅜λ folded feed stub, fed at the bottom = 11/16λ total. No ground plane needed (self-resonant), ~1 dB over a λ/4, easy to build from a single wire. The go-to portable/mobile FM vertical.
5/8-wave5/8λ vertical over a ground plane (radials). ~2–3 dB gain over λ/4, slightly lower feedpoint impedance — the standard for fixed VHF/UHF base stations and repeaters.
FT8Weak-signal digital (WSJT-X) — 6-tone FSK, ~6.7 Hz bandwidth, 15 s cycles. The default HF digimode.
FT4Faster version of FT8 — 7.5 s cycles, 4 FSK tones.
WSPRVery weak-signal propagation (WSJT-X) — 4 FSK tones, 2.5 Hz bandwidth, 2-min cycles. Listen in the CW segment; TX anywhere in the narrowband sub-band.
PSK31Phase-shift keying, 31 baud — keyboard data, ~31 Hz bandwidth. Popular on 40/20 m.
RTTYRipple Telegraphy — FSK 170 Hz shift, 45 baud. TX frequency given is the mark (audio 1700 Hz).
AMTOR / HellschreiberOlder robust digimodes — still on the band plan, rarely used now.
DMRDigital Mobile Radio — 2m/70cm digital voice, 4.5 kHz, BrandMeister/DMRplus repeater network.
C4FMC4FM digital voice (Yaesu Fusion / Icom IDAS) — 6.25 kHz, 2 m.
AX.25 / APRSPacket radio / Automatic Position Reporting — 1200 baud AFSK on 145.525 MHz (UK).
MGMMachine-Generated Mode — umbrella for computer-driven digimodes.
CoACentre of Activity — recommended calling frequency for a mode/segment.
SSTVSlow-Scan TV — image transmission, 40 m (7.175 MHz), 20 m (14.230 MHz).
DSCDigital Selective Calling — marine digital distress/calling (Ch 16 / 85B).
ADS-BAutomatic Dependent Surveillance-Broadcast — aircraft position via Mode S 1090 MHz.
